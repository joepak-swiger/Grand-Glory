# Prototype gameplay

## Visual stage

The `City Street (Prototype)` stage is an 1800×1200 neighborhood with a broad east-west boulevard, a second cross street, two north-south avenues, sidewalks, four marked intersections, and six coherent building footprints. Shared geometry in `src/game/stage.ts` drives the rendered map, walkability, bounds, spawning, collision, projectiles, and enemy route field. Asphalt and road paint are authored separately from the atlas because its road crops contain repeated baked lane lines. Building roofs use consistent original brick, glass, and industrial palettes; the CC0 atlas supplies pavement and sidewalk textures.

Buildings are solid rectangular footprints. Players, enemies, and dashes stop at those footprints and can slide along their edges; collision is swept in bounded substeps. Projectiles stop at buildings, and automatic attacks require a clear shot. The Gang Leader and ordinary enemies share one breadth-first flow field, rebuilt when Blue Pinwheel enters a new 32-unit stage cell. Roads, sidewalks, and open ground are walkable. Street lamps and paint are decorative and nonblocking. Press F4 in development to compare collision cells and actor radii with the rendered city.

## Controls

- Move: WASD / arrow keys
- Dash: Shift or Space
- Prototype special: Q (earned through combat)
- Pause/resume: Escape
- Upgrade/menu selection: click or Enter

Blue Pinwheel and both thug sprite strips use supplied right-facing source art. While the player or an enemy deliberately moves left/right, its sprite turns horizontally to match. Vertical movement and idle retain the last horizontal facing (right at run start). Knockback never determines enemy facing. Boss facing follows pursuit or its locked attack aim. This uses mirroring only; no authentic front/back animation frames are currently available.

The Gang Leader has no supplied character art. It uses a larger original four-pose pixel silhouette as a temporary prototype, not an owner character design; it enters from a safe visible horizontal position and its facing follows pursuit and locked attack aim. The Bruiser still uses a temporary generated four-pose silhouette with a broad armored frame. The Street Pitcher uses owner-supplied right-facing sprite art. Supplied right-facing thug sprites and the hero's right-facing profile mirror only the individual character sprite when facing left.

## Run and prototype balance

The standard run targets three minutes before continuing into Endless Mode. Ordinary population targets rise from 8 at the start to 24 at 15 seconds, 40 at 30 seconds, 64 at one minute, 82 at 90 seconds, 104 at two minutes, and 120 at the 2:30 boss arrival. The director estimates spawn throughput from the current deficit, recent kill rate, and surge state. The average rate is capped at 14 newly spawned enemies per second, with a four-enemy per-update budget and four-token catch-up ceiling; a small bounded opening budget creates the first group promptly. The ordinary cap is 128. When the crowd reaches its target, the stored catch-up budget is cleared. These are targets, not promises: actual populations depend on kills, placement safety, and the boss-phase cap. Spawns stay near the camera's street edge and at least 180 world units from the player; distant stragglers are repositioned if they stay farther than 760 units for 4.5 seconds.

Every 35 seconds before the first boss, a ten-second **HORDE SURGE** announces its incoming compass direction. Its groups enter from that side, add up to 12 enemies to the director target, and favor fast thugs and Street Pitchers with a small Bruiser share. The side is selected from safe directions; there are no surprise spawns beside Blue Pinwheel. The Gang Boss arrives at 2:30. In Endless Mode, defeating a boss advances the faction encounter and drops a persistent boss cache; collecting it pauses combat for an evolution choice or a useful recovery fallback. Boss defeats do not end Endless Mode. The finite test mode preserves its encounter-completion path. Losing all health ends the run immediately.

Blue Pinwheel starts at 160 HP. **Normal** is the default and applies 0.72× incoming damage, 0.88× hostile projectile speed, a 1.12× ranged attack interval, 1.05 seconds of post-hit invulnerability, and a 2.7-second dash cooldown. **Heroic** uses the prior baseline: full damage/speed, 0.8-second invulnerability, and a 3-second dash cooldown. Enemy HP, XP, and the standard horde target curve are identical between modes. These values are prototype balance, not canon. Level-up choices use six weapon slots and six universal Heroic Trait slots. Regeneration adds 0.2 HP/s per rank; the old Recovery, Armor Plating, and Prototype Power upgrade IDs are retired to avoid duplicate stats. Weapon evolutions require a maximum-rank weapon plus the matching trait and a collected boss cache. Results report actual damage taken by source. `?test=1` uses a shorter deterministic encounter for browser verification. `?test=normal` runs the ordinary configuration with development-only browser diagnostics. F3 toggles the diagnostics panel; its **Copy run diagnostics** button copies a compact run summary when browser clipboard access is available.

Blue Pinwheel's **Starter Ring Throw (Prototype)** automatically throws a spinning metallic-blue ring at the nearest enemy. **Orbit Guard** uses distinct moving ring entities; its timed active cycle is lengthened by Persistence, and Eternal Halo removes the normal downtime. **Piercing Ring** throws a visibly distinct ring that pierces enemies according to its rank. The original ring textures are renderer-created assets guided by owner-provided descriptions; no supplied raw reference image is republished. Tempo reduces compatible weapon cooldowns and Multiplicity adds bounded projectiles to compatible attacks. Blue Pinwheel can also unlock **Ricochet Rings**, which reflect from stage buildings and boundaries a finite number of times. Prototype Special remains a temporary area attack. Upgrade cards show the current and next skill rank and concrete effects. Typhoon Rings requires maximum Starter Ring rank and Might, and is chosen from a collected boss cache rather than an ordinary level-up card. All prototype weapons/evolutions remain non-canon gameplay until the owner confirms otherwise.

Bok-Goo's build includes **Club Sweep**, **Boulder Toss**, and **Ground Slam**. Boulder Toss launches an arcing stone volley, gains a second stone at rank 3, and gains a small impact splash at rank 4. Club Sweep performs a timed second swing at rank 4. Da-Ri's build includes **Spear Thrust**, **Spear Toss**, and **Spear Sweep**; Spear Thrust performs a timed follow-up at rank 4, while Spear Toss gains a second spear at rank 4. Their max-rank/passive boss-cache evolutions are Earthbreaker, Avalanche Toss, Obsidian Tempest, and Returning Lance. All evolution names and mechanics are gameplay prototypes.

The thirteen universal Heroic Traits are Might, Tempo, Reach, Momentum, Persistence, Multiplicity, Resilience, Vitality, Regeneration, Agility, Attraction, Fortune, and Wisdom. Each has a typed cap and concrete effect. Weapon/trait compatibility is defined in the registry; traits do not change unrelated attack statistics. Build ranks and weapon slot counts are visible in upgrade cards and the combat HUD.

Unarmed Thugs pursue and damage Blue Pinwheel at melee range. Fast Thugs retain their supplied bat-wielding design and attack within short range after a 0.32-second warning, with a short visible swing cue and recovery window; they do not throw projectiles. Bruisers are slower, durable melee threats. The former provisional Gangster slot is now the **Street Pitcher**, using the owner's four-frame walk cycle and throw pose to telegraph and throw one baseball. Ranged attacks are opt-in per enemy definition; none of the current unarmed, bat-wielding, or Bruiser definitions can shoot. The unnamed Gang Leader's separate prototype projectile and charge attacks remain unchanged. Prototype enemy separation prevents complete pile-ups. The selected difficulty sets player-wide invulnerability (1.05 seconds on Normal; 0.8 seconds on Heroic), preventing repeated or simultaneous contact from draining health every frame.

Enemy defeats drop visible XP gems at a nearby walkable point. Gems stay at their defeat locations until the player approaches; the default direct collection radius is 24 world units and the short-range attraction radius is 72 units. Attraction accelerates smoothly only inside that radius and only with clear line of sight. Wide Pickup adds 20 world units per rank, capped at 160. Nearby gem piles within 20 units may merge only when no building separates them and their value-weighted position is walkable; the total XP value is conserved. XP is awarded only when gems are collected. The F3 development panel and copied run summary report XP spawned, collected, on-map, merged, and lost.

A level-up pauses simulation until one of three distinct eligible upgrades is chosen by click or keys 1–3. Every card shows a prominent `LEVEL n → LEVEL n+1` or `NEW · LEVEL 1` skill-rank label; this is distinct from the overall player level. An eligible weapon choice, survival/utility choice, and combat-stat choice are offered when those pools have options. Upgrade choices track ranks and filter capped options. The boss phase reduces the regular horde cap to 68% of the configured cap.

The Gang Leader alternates an aimed projectile, a wind-up charge, and recovery. A highlighted line shows the projectile's locked aim or charge lane before it begins. Normal reduces incoming boss damage and slows hostile shots; the charge deals one hit if it intersects the player. Damage source totals distinguish ordinary/fast/bruiser contact, ranged shots, boss shots, and boss charges. All boss and weapon attacks remain temporary prototype mechanics.

## Assignment 010 — heroes, factions, and endless survival

The title screen offers Blue Pinwheel, Bok-Goo (복구 · The Boulder), and Da-Ri (다리 · The Obsidian); selection changes the player texture, maximum health, movement speed, starter attack, and eligible hero-specific ranks. Blue Pinwheel retains his established 160 HP / 210 speed blue ring throw. Bok-Goo starts at 225 HP / 180 speed with an auto-aimed directional Club Sweep; Da-Ri starts at 145 HP / 225 speed with a long, narrow, piercing Spear Thrust. Both new starters have five functional ranks, and Boulder Toss or Spear Toss is an unlockable secondary. After the Gang Boss encounter, the director initially favors the newly introduced Dr. Smile faction at 65% and retains Street Gang enemies at 35%. Later encounters continue mixing the available factions under the existing enemy cap. All these moves and names are provisional gameplay mechanics, not confirmed canon. Da-Ri's source art is a right-facing pose sequence only; mirroring left is a rendering fallback, not an authored alternate view.

The default mode is Endless. The Gang Boss is scheduled first at the existing 2:30 target. His current prototype attacks are Chain Cyclone (telegraphed radial swing) and Chain Throw (a visible tether and returning spiked ball). Defeating him awards XP, increments the encounter/wave counters, leaves the run active, and schedules Dr. Smile for no earlier than 5:00 and at least 30 seconds after the first victory. After the first boss, Smilers and Molars can join the Street Gang roster at a configured 65% faction roll, so older enemies remain in the mix. Dr. Smile's prototype attacks are a directional dental-tool lunge and a telegraphed hazard zone. Later implemented boss choices alternate deterministically between the two current bosses; health increases are capped. Tiger Mom, Dr. Eggplant, and Clownasaurus Rex are registered as disabled plans only and cannot be selected.

## Grand Arsenal — consolidated Assignment 011

Weapon options come from the typed registry; each active weapon has five functional ranks and a hero owner. A run has six weapon slots and six distinct Heroic Trait slots. Starting weapons occupy slots; rank-ups do not. Traits are universal and shared across all heroes: Might, Tempo, Reach, Momentum, Persistence, Multiplicity, Resilience, Vitality, Regeneration, Agility, Attraction, Fortune, and Wisdom. Traits have explicit rank caps and affect meaningful combat statistics. Multiplicity is capped at two ranks; projectile/attack counts also have per-weapon upper bounds. Mastery remains an endless-only, bounded post-cap upgrade.

The seven boss-cache evolutions require rank 5 in their base weapon and at least rank 1 of the matching trait: Starter Rings + Might → Typhoon Rings; Orbit Guard + Persistence → Eternal Halo; Ricochet Rings + Resilience → Shatterstorm; Club Sweep + Reach → Earthbreaker; Boulder Toss + Multiplicity → Avalanche Toss; Spear Thrust + Tempo → Obsidian Tempest; Spear Toss + Momentum → Returning Lance. A boss reward does not consume the trait. Evolved weapon names replace their base names in the active build display. Fortune has a seeded, bounded chance to improve a non-evolution cache; it cannot satisfy an evolution prerequisite. The complete research-to-archetype mapping is in [WEAPON_INSPIRATION_ATLAS.md](WEAPON_INSPIRATION_ATLAS.md).

Yo-Yo Punk is the Street Gang's owner-art ranged-control prototype: she locks an aim point, telegraphs, extends a visible yo-yo line, holds the line as a collision hazard, then retracts and recovers. The tether checks the full segment and only deals damage during its active hold phase with a cooldown. The supplied Street Pitcher remains the only ordinary ballistic thrower, using baseball shots. Unarmed thugs, bat thugs, Bruisers, Smilers, and Molars pursue and use contact or short melee; Smilers and Molars do not fire projectiles.

Pause includes **Retire Run**. Endless results identify the hero, difficulty, survival time, player level, kills, bosses and waves, encountered factions, peak horde, actual damage dealt, incoming damage by source, XP collected/on-map, and the final weapon build. Retiring is available from pause and does not count as a victory. The short `?test=1` run remains a finite regression path; only that finite mode ends in victory when its boss is defeated. Endless upgrade choices offer a bounded repeatable +5% damage Mastery after normal ranks are exhausted, capped at 20 Mastery ranks and +100% from that upgrade.

## Camera and sound

Choose Wide (0.94×), Standard (1.08×, default), or Close (1.2×) on the title screen. This scales only the world layer; screen-space UI and all collision radii remain unchanged. The camera setting is saved locally. Synthesized sound effects begin after the first user interaction; mute and SFX volume persist locally. First launch defaults to 55% volume; a saved zero remains silent. Cues cover menus, attacks, hits, defeats, pickups, dash, special, boss arrival, and run outcomes, with repeated hit/pickup/defeat sounds throttled. Reduced Motion suppresses rapid hit flashing, most special-ring movement, and UI transitions.

Starter and Piercing Ring projectiles use blue metallic ring textures with hollow centers, bright highlights, and restrained blue trails; Piercing Ring uses an overlapping twin-ring silhouette. Orbit Guard uses the same blue-and-silver visual language. XP gems use small cyan-green, medium blue, and large violet crystals based on their accumulated value. The Street Pitcher appears in its supplied walk cycle and uses the still throwing pose during its readable baseball wind-up. Aim warnings and boss arrival cues remain visible without freezing the run.

## Reward feedback — Assignment 013

Recovery, XP Recall, Curry, supply-box, elite-cache, and boss-reward events use brief pickup toasts. Supply cache choices have a gold-accented opening presentation and remain paused until selection; after resuming, the toast is short and sits above the arena HUD. XP Recall adds an expanding cyan signal for about 1.35 seconds. It does not change the underlying XP value, Wisdom application, or blocked-building handling. The feedback is intentionally bounded and follows Reduced Motion.
# Assignment 012 — Supply Drops and Elites

The city now contains four nonblocking supply boxes that can be broken by player projectiles. Recovery Rations restore `max(35 HP, 20% max HP)` up to the health cap; they stay on the ground while the hero is at full health. Rare XP Recall calls all outstanding XP piles back to the hero and credits their exact stored value once per pile, with Wisdom applied at collection. Inferno Curry is an eight-second, forward-facing cone attack shared by every hero, with per-target damage cooldowns. These are generic pickups, not new canonical hero powers.

The Horde Director schedules an initial elite at 42 seconds and later attempts every 58 seconds, capped at two active elites. Elites retain their enemy's normal silhouette and behavior, gain 2× health, 1.12× damage, 1.5× XP, a gold star/health bar, and knockback resistance. Every spawned elite reserves a bounded cache slot and leaves a collectible Field or Hero Cache on defeat. Caches pause the run for an eligible upgrade choice; Hero Caches also grant a small recovery/special bonus. Boss evolution rewards remain on the existing boss cache path.

Normal utility drop chances and Fortune scaling are documented in [PICKUPS.md](PICKUPS.md). Heroic uses 55% of the Normal utility odds. These additions are an initial tuning pass; the automated results should not be treated as owner human playtesting.
