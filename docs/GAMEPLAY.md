# Prototype gameplay

## Visual stage

The `City Street (Prototype)` stage is an 1800×1200 neighborhood with a broad east-west boulevard, a second cross street, two north-south avenues, sidewalks, four marked intersections, and six coherent building footprints. Shared geometry in `src/game/stage.ts` drives the rendered map, walkability, bounds, spawning, collision, projectiles, and enemy route field. Asphalt and road paint are authored separately from the atlas because its road crops contain repeated baked lane lines. Building roofs use consistent original brick, glass, and industrial palettes; the CC0 atlas supplies pavement and sidewalk textures.

Buildings are solid rectangular footprints. Players, enemies, and dashes stop at those footprints and can slide along their edges; collision is swept in bounded substeps. Projectiles stop at buildings, and automatic attacks require a clear shot. The Gang Leader and ordinary enemies share one breadth-first flow field, rebuilt when Blue Pinwheel enters a new 32-unit stage cell. Roads, sidewalks, and open ground are walkable. Street lamps and paint are decorative and nonblocking. Press F4 in development to compare collision cells and actor radii with the rendered city.

## Controls

- Move: WASD / arrow keys
- Dash: Shift or Space
- Prototype special: Q (earned through combat)
- Pause/resume: Escape
- Upgrade/menu selection: click or Enter

Blue Pinwheel and both thug sprite strips use supplied right-facing source art. While the player or an enemy deliberately moves left/right, its sprite turns horizontally to match. Vertical movement and idle retain the last horizontal facing (right at run start). Knockback never determines enemy facing. Boss facing follows pursuit or its locked attack aim. This uses mirroring only; no authentic front/back animation frames are currently available.

The Gang Leader has no supplied character art and remains a geometric gameplay placeholder with a small directional cue; it enters from a safe visible horizontal position and its facing follows pursuit and locked attack aim. Supplied right-facing thug sprites and the hero's right-facing profile mirror only the individual character sprite when facing left.

## Run and prototype balance

The standard run targets three minutes. Ordinary population targets rise from 8 at the start to 24 at 15 seconds, 40 at 30 seconds, 64 at one minute, 82 at 90 seconds, 104 at two minutes, and 120 at the 2:30 boss arrival. The director estimates spawn throughput from the current deficit, recent kill rate, and surge state. The average rate is capped at 14 newly spawned enemies per second, with a four-enemy per-update budget and four-token catch-up ceiling; a small bounded opening budget creates the first group promptly. The ordinary cap is 128. When the crowd reaches its target, the stored catch-up budget is cleared. These are targets, not promises: actual populations depend on kills, placement safety, and the boss-phase cap. Spawns stay near the camera's street edge and at least 180 world units from the player; distant stragglers are repositioned if they stay farther than 760 units for 4.5 seconds.

Every 35 seconds before the boss, a ten-second **HORDE SURGE** announces its incoming compass direction. Its groups enter from that side, add up to 12 enemies to the director target, and favor fast thugs and ranged Gangsters with a small Bruiser share. The side is selected from safe directions; there are no surprise spawns beside Blue Pinwheel. Boss-phase ordinary target pressure is 68% of the ordinary cap. Existing enemies are not deleted when the phase begins; new spawning pauses until the count falls under the adjusted target. The Gang Leader arrives at 2:30 with 460 HP. He now cycles through a projectile warning and a charge more often (6.2-second attack cycle); the first projectile warning begins about one second after arrival. Defeating him ends the run in victory. At 3:00 the HUD labels the encounter **OVERTIME**; the boss fight continues for up to one additional minute. If the boss is still alive at 4:00, the Gang Leader escapes and the run ends in defeat. Losing all health ends the run immediately.

Blue Pinwheel starts at 160 HP; ordinary contact damage is Thug 5, Fast Thug 4, Gangster 4, Bruiser 10, with 0.8 seconds of player-wide contact invulnerability. Level-up choices still separate weapons, survival/utility, and combat stats, but vitality and recovery are no longer forced into each offered set. An unranked automatic weapon is prioritized so new attack paths can come online; cards show the next weapon rank's damage effect. `?test=1` uses a shorter deterministic encounter for browser verification. `?test=normal` runs the ordinary configuration with development-only browser diagnostics. F3 toggles the diagnostics panel; its **Copy run diagnostics** button copies a compact run summary when browser clipboard access is available.

Blue Pinwheel's **Pinwheel (Prototype)** automatically throws rotating pinwheel-shaped geometric projectiles at the nearest enemy. **Orbit Guard (Prototype)** is a short-range recurring attack; **Piercing Throw (Prototype)** is a separate, automatic piercing projectile. **Prototype Special — Canon TBD** is a temporary area attack. Weapon rank cards strengthen attacks through five ranks. Pinwheel Evolution is offered once only after Pinwheel rank 5 and Prototype Power rank 2; it adds piercing and faster firing. This entire kit is a gameplay experiment, not canon.

Thug and Fast Thug enemies pursue the player and deal one full contact-damage hit when contact begins. Gangsters keep their distance and telegraph a three-shot projectile fan. Bruisers have high health and contact damage but move slowly. Prototype enemy separation prevents complete pile-ups. Player-wide 0.8-second invulnerability prevents repeated or simultaneous contact from draining health every frame. Enemies spawn in safe camera-adjacent street regions and drop XP; nearby XP piles merge without losing value and drift toward Blue Pinwheel within collection range. A level-up pauses simulation until one of three distinct eligible upgrades is chosen by click or keys 1–3; an eligible weapon choice, survival/utility choice, and combat-stat choice are offered when those pools have options. Upgrade choices track ranks and filter capped options. The boss phase reduces the regular horde cap to 68% of the configured cap.

The Gang Leader alternates an aimed projectile, a wind-up charge, and recovery. A highlighted line shows the projectile's locked aim or the charge lane before it begins. The charge deals one hit if it intersects the player; the projectile has a collision radius that includes its visible body. A high-contrast outline and draw priority help distinguish the temporary geometric boss placeholder from ordinary enemies. All boss and weapon attacks remain temporary prototype mechanics.
